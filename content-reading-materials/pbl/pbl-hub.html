The project cycle

Every project follows the same five steps. Use them as a checkpoint list, and loop back as often as your students need.

1

Launch

Open with an entry event, such as a photo, a problem, or a guest, and post a driving question.

2

Build knowledge

Teach the skills and content students need, just in time, as they ask for it.

3

Create

Students draft, test, and revise a product that answers the driving question.

4

Share

Present to a real audience, like families, another class, or a community partner.

5

Reflect

Students look back on what they learned, what they would change, and what comes next.

Gold Standard PBL elements

Based on the Buck Institute for Education framework. Each element has a key idea and the session tools that support it. Learning goals sit at the center: key knowledge, understanding, and success skills.

Challenging problem or question

Use an open-ended question that captures a real issue. Avoid questions students can answer with a quick search.

Tools: developing questions chart (Question, Who, Action, For)

Sustained inquiry

Students research over time, log what they find, and return to their questions as new ones appear.

Tools: inquiry log, project tracker, source check (author, purpose, bias, credibility)

Authenticity

Connect the work to students' lives, their families, and the wider world.

Tools: 3 Whys (me, people around me, the world), thinking visible routine

Student voice and choice

Offer a menu of options so students shape how they work, what they research, and how they present.

Tools: choice board, working groups, presentation format options

Reflection

Build in reflection at checkpoints. Use a mix of self, peer, and teacher feedback.

Tools: Glows and Grows, 3-2-1 reflection, Rock Paper Scissors reflection

Critique and revision

Teach students to give kind, specific feedback and to revise based on it.

Tools: TAG feedback, I like / I wonder / What if protocol, feedback guide

Public product

Share final work with a real audience, such as families, another class, or a community partner.

Tools: choice board (record, sketch, present), poster session, round table, podcast

Planning tools from the sessions also include backward design questions, a project contract, group roles, and a monthly calendar to start the project.

Project planning checklist

Check these off before, during, and after each project.

Before

Plan the project

Name the standards you will teach and the skills students will practice.

Write a driving question that students actually care about.

Decide on the final product and how it will be shared.

Set checkpoints and a due date for each one.

During

Run the project

Give students a clear way to track progress, like a team log.

Hold short check-ins and give specific feedback on drafts.

Teach mini-lessons when you see a need, not on a fixed schedule.

Keep a list of questions students raise for future units.

After

Close the loop

Score the project against your standards using a shared rubric.

Ask students to reflect on their process, not just their product.

Note what worked and what you would change next time.

Share the final work with families or your school community.
